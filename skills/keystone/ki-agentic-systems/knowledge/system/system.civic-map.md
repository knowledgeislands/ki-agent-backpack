# The civic model, by analogy

This card helps a newcomer grasp the shape of Knowledge Islands' work model quickly, by setting it beside how older civilisations organised the same things: where people meet, who heads a province, how word travels and where records are kept. It is an analogy, never a definition. The system is its own synthesis, taking what each source did well without adopting any one of them, or its prejudices, and its own names come from things in the landscape that outlast their makers, not from a civilisation and its time (NAMING-RULE).

The work model's words are defined on their own cards: the [work package][system.work-package], each a node of the work tree, the [role][system.role] and the [seat][system.seat].

| In Knowledge Islands | By analogy | Where it holds | Where it breaks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Cairn, the store where sessions meet | The Athenian agora and the Roman forum, the open square at the centre of a city's public life | The one shared place where everyone meets, leaves word and sees what is going on; each meeting's message is a file there | Nothing is traded or owned there, and it is durable, which is why the Agora took a landscape name, the Cairn |
| The guardian, keeping the state of every package | The agoranomoi, a board of ten chosen by lot each year, five for the city and five for Piraeus, who policed what was sold in the Athenian market (Aristotle, _Ath. Pol._ 51); the guardian was called the agoranomos until 11 November 2026 | A loose match: a keeper of order over the common place | It is one keeper, not a board, and nothing is sold in the Cairn; it commands no one, does no package's own work and acts on no change document before the owner's word |
| A territory and its governor | A Persian satrapy and its satrap; a Roman province and its governor | One head for each territory, answering to the centre | There is no tribute, army or conquest; a governor heads work, and the centre is the owner, not a throne; and where Rome ranked consular legates above praetorian ones, no governor here outranks another |
| A territory's council, its governor and any councillors | A Roman governor's consilium, the advisers who sat with him to hear cases and settle his province's business | A territory's head and those beside it settling its business together | A consilium only advised its governor, where the council decides together, and the owner can overturn any of its decisions |
| Steer sent down the tree; feedback, queries and escalations sent up | Chinese imperial edicts sent down, and officials' memorials sent up to the throne | Decisions go down, and what cannot be settled below goes up in writing | Memorials went straight to the throne, and the Qing palace memorials were made to bypass the officials between; here what a level cannot settle goes to the one above, not straight to the owner. A message carries no authority of its own; only the owner's quoted word does |
| Work reaching a package along the tree | The Persian Royal Road's relay couriers, the angareion, each rider handing the message on at the next station | Word passes node to node, and each node takes what has reached it when it next wakes | Nothing is carried or pushed: messages are files each session reads, so the store is not a [channel][industry.channel] |
| Git history and the store's `record/` | Rome's tabularium, the building on the Capitol traditionally identified as the state record office, a reading Purcell and Tucci dispute | An archive where what was done is kept | The archive is the history itself, open to every session, and never rewritten |
| The housekeeper, keeping one machine in repair | Rome's curator aquarum, who kept the city's aqueducts in repair | A keeper of the fabric others work in | It keeps one machine, not a city's water, and answers to the owner, not an emperor |

## Where the analogy stops

Empires ran on conquest, tribute and rank by birth. Knowledge Islands takes none of that: every institution in it exists to serve its purpose and the people it serves, never to protect its members' standing, the prime directive of its ethical guilds. Read each row for the shape it shows, and the card it links for what the thing is.
