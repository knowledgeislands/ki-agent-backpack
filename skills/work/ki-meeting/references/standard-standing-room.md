## 1. Introduction

A standing room is the type of meeting that runs while its members work, with no time limit per sitting, and closes only on the owner's word (MEET-STANDING, Kris, 18 November 2026). It runs by [the meeting standard](standard-meeting.md); this standard adds only what a standing room is for and where it differs. Two rooms were held this way before the type was set: the housekeepers' room between two rigs, which closed on 20 November 2026 once Orca mail crossed the bridge between them (ROOMS-CHANNEL), and the owner's voice room. The decisions behind these rules are in [the sources file](sources.md#3-decisions).

## 4. Rules

The rules, part by part.

### 4.1 What a standing room is for

1. **A standing conversation with a seat outside Orca,** where that need lasts beyond one matter, such as the owner's voice room, where a voice client and the guardian talk while the owner thinks things through out loud. A room is optional and is set up mainly to reach such a seat (ROOMS-OPTIONAL and ORCA-HOME, [the meeting standard, 4.1](standard-meeting.md#41-when-to-meet)).
2. **Never a route between sessions in Orca.** They reach each other by Orca mail, on the same Mac and across the bridge between rigs (ROOMS-CHANNEL, [the meeting standard, 4.6](standard-meeting.md#46-routes)), so rigs no longer need a standing room, and one held only as a route closes.
3. **Otherwise, another type.** Work on one matter, for a while, is a workshop, review or retrospective, which ends.

### 4.2 What a standing room produces

1. **Messages and minutes, nothing else.** A standing room needs no `brief/` and no output folder; its entry file's brief says why it stands, and anything too long for a message goes in `notes/`.
2. **A decision still goes to a change document.** The room holds the conversation, never the decision ([the meeting standard, 4.13](standard-meeting.md#413-decisions)); the brief names the change documents its decisions go to.

### 4.3 Holding a standing room

1. **Sittings without a time limit.** A standing room runs in sittings, one for each spell of work or each talk. A sitting states no end and has no closing call; the caller adjourns it when its members stop work, as the voice room does, and opens a new one when work resumes. So a member reads as listening only while a sitting is under way ([the meeting standard, 4.9](standard-meeting.md#49-listening-and-check-ins)).
2. **The roll call** opens each sitting as in any meeting; a member that starts work while a sitting is under way answers it when it starts.
3. **Members listen while they work,** at the interval the agenda gives each, and not between; the caller's checks back off and stop after an hour of quiet, as [the meeting standard, 4.9](standard-meeting.md#49-listening-and-check-ins) sets (LISTEN-BACKOFF).
4. **Messages, check-ins and minutes** keep the meeting standard's shape. Check-ins carry news only ([the meeting standard, 4.9](standard-meeting.md#49-listening-and-check-ins)).
5. **Pruning.** Each sitting's messages go when it adjourns, and in a sitting that runs on, those answered or acknowledged more than 24 hours ago go too, under [the meeting standard, 4.17](standard-meeting.md#417-pruning).

### 4.4 Closing a standing room

1. **Only on the owner's word.** A standing room closes when the owner says so, never because its work has run out; the caller then closes it as [the meeting standard, 4.15](standard-meeting.md#415-closing) sets.
